Supplier invoice price variance: how to check it

A price variance appears when the billed price per unit differs from the rate authorized on a purchase order or contract. Even small differences can matter on repeated wholesale purchases.

What this check means

A price variance appears when the billed price per unit differs from the rate authorized on a purchase order or contract. Even small differences can matter on repeated wholesale purchases.

What records you need

Ensure every line includes an unambiguous PO reference, SKU and a unit price in the same currency. Compare the vendor invoice unit price with the authorized PO unit price, using a stated tolerance for rounding. Include the purchased and billed quantities so differences can be estimated but not mistaken for confirmed refunds.

What to investigate

A legitimate price adjustment, promotional allowance, vendor credit or weighted average for multiple PO lines can affect the result. If invoices and POs use cases versus individual units, normalize the unit of measure before comparing.

Frequently asked question

Is a price variance always an overcharge?

No. Verify amendments to the PO, discounts, taxes, freight, partial shipments, credits and supplier contracts before challenging a bill.
