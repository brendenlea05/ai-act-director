Receiving controls / quantities

What to do when the invoice quantity exceeds the received quantity

An invoice for 20 units does not necessarily mean your warehouse has accepted 20 units. Comparing billed quantities with goods receipts is a useful way to catch timing and quantity problems before the accounting team closes an invoice.

Example: 20 billed, 18 accepted

Suppose an authorized purchase order is for 20 units. The receiving log shows 18 accepted units and the vendor invoice bills all 20 at $31 per unit. The two-unit difference represents $62 in invoiced value requiring review. It is not automatically money owed back: the remaining units may be in transit or the receipt may be posted after the report cutoff.

Check the operational causes first

  1. Confirm the warehouse's accepted quantity, including damaged or rejected goods, backorders, and multiple receipts.
  2. Check the invoice and receipt dates. Use the same cutoff window across the three exports.
  3. Verify unit conversions, especially cases, pallets, eaches and partial packs.
  4. Review credit notes, supplier packing slips, revised orders and short-shipment documentation.
  5. Record the decision so another reviewer doesn't reopen the same exception without context.

What to put in your review notes

Include the invoice ID, PO, SKU, quantity ordered, quantity accepted, quantity billed and the date range of the receiving extract. If a quantity is missing, note whether the warehouse is confirming a delivery or the supplier has been asked to clarify the bill.
