Three-way matching: what to check before you pay a supplier

A three-way match compares the terms on the purchase order, the goods the business recorded as received, and the quantities and prices on the supplier invoice. When they disagree, the goal is to find out why before anyone treats the difference as an approved charge or an overpayment.

Start with the right records

Export each source at the line-item level. A useful purchase-order extract has a PO number, SKU, authorized quantity and agreed unit price. A receiving extract needs the PO number, SKU and quantity accepted. Your invoice extract needs an invoice number, PO number, SKU, billed quantity and unit price. Supplier and currency fields help when multiple vendors or currencies appear in one export.

Worked example

Suppose PO-1041 authorizes 20 units at $28 each. The warehouse has accepted 18 units, but the supplier invoices 20 units at $31 each.

RecordQuantityUnit priceWhat it establishes
Purchase order20$28Authorized order terms
Goods receipt18—Accepted units in this extract
Vendor invoice20$31Amount billed by supplier

The billed unit price is $3 higher, a nominal $60 difference on 20 billed units. Two units were billed beyond the receipt quantity, worth $62 at the invoiced rate. Those amounts overlap and should not simply be added together. The invoice might be premature, the receipt may be incomplete, or the price may have changed legitimately. These are questions to resolve, not proof of an overcharge.

A practical review sequence

  1. Match the identifiers. Check PO number and SKU, then confirm the units of measure. A case of 12 is not one unit.
  2. Check the date cutoff. Goods received after your export date may explain an apparent shortage.
  3. Compare the agreed terms. Look for order amendments, volume tiers, rebates and supplier credits.
  4. Review repeats. Identical invoice lines can be duplicated imports or valid split lines. Confirm against the source invoice.
  5. Record a decision. Note the evidence, responsible reviewer and next action. Send the vendor a specific question only after checking internally.

Frequently asked question

Can three-way matching prevent every overpayment?

No. A three-way match is an important control but does not verify contract rebates, taxes, freight, exchange rates, credits, payment history or whether an invoice is fraudulent.
