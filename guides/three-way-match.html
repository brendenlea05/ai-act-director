Three-way matching for supplier invoices: practical guide

Three-way matching checks whether the quantities and prices agreed on a purchase order correspond to goods accepted and the supplier invoice.

What this check means

Three-way matching checks whether the quantities and prices agreed on a purchase order correspond to goods accepted and the supplier invoice.

What records you need

Start by exporting purchase-order lines with PO number, SKU, authorized quantity and agreed unit cost. Next, export receiving records with the same PO and SKU; quantities should represent accepted goods rather than goods merely ordered. Finally, export the supplier invoice lines with invoice identifier, PO number, SKU, billed quantity and billed unit price.

What to investigate

Look for invoices billed above accepted quantities, unit prices exceeding purchase-order prices, invoices with no matching PO and lines repeated under the same invoice identifier. These are prompts for investigation: a delivery may have arrived after the export date, a discount may be applied later or the supplier might use a different unit of measure.

Frequently asked question

Can three-way matching prevent every overpayment?

No. A three-way match is an important control but does not verify contract rebates, taxes, freight, exchange rates, credits, payment history or whether an invoice is fraudulent.
