How to reconcile purchase orders and vendor bills without a new integration

For a small purchasing team, a quick CSV review can be useful when supplier invoices are coming from different systems or a full procurement implementation isn't on the table. The important part is agreeing on identifiers and cutoffs before comparing totals.

Export these three reports

  1. Purchase-order lines: one row per authorized PO/SKU quantity and unit price. Retain the PO number and supplier identity.
  2. Goods-receipt lines: the same PO and SKU, with quantities actually accepted. Don't substitute quantities ordered for quantities received.
  3. Vendor invoice lines: invoice number, referenced PO, SKU, billed quantity and billed unit price. Include the currency if you can.

Prepare the data before comparing

Remove duplicate report headers, standardize inconsistent SKU formats, and ensure that line-level quantities are expressed in comparable units. Keep a copy of the original exports: a clean review is only useful if someone can trace a flagged row back to the source.

What the first pass should reveal

A useful check identifies invoices without a matching PO, higher-than-authorized unit prices, billed quantities above accepted receipts, and possibly repeated invoice lines. It should also distinguish open orders and missing receiving records from definite price or quantity problems.

For example, an invoice under PO-1041 might refer to a SKU that never appears on the uploaded purchase order. The mismatch could be an unauthorized purchase, but it might also be a reporting period issue or a different item code. Confirm the explanation before escalating.
